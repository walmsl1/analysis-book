Theorem 0.6.8 Continuous functions keep their sign nearby.

Suppose \(f\) is continuous at \(a\) and \(f(a)\gt 0\text{.}\) Then there exists \(\delta\gt 0\) such that for all \(x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\) with \(|x-a|\lt\delta\text{,}\) \(f(x)\gt 0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Assume \(f\) is continuous at \(a\) and \(f(a)\gt 0\text{.}\) We must prove that there exists \(\delta\gt 0\) such that for all \(x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\) with \(|x-a|\lt\delta\text{,}\) \(f(x)\gt 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Because \(f\) is continuous at \(a\) and \(f(a)\gt 0\text{,}\) by the \(\epsilon\)–\(\delta\) property there exists \(\delta\gt 0\) such that for all \(x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\) with \(|x-a|\lt\delta\text{,}\) \(|f(x)-f(a)|\lt f(a)\text{.}\) With this \(\delta\text{,}\) we will show that for all \(x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\) with \(|x-a|\lt\delta\text{,}\) \(f(x)\gt 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Let \(x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\) with \(|x-a|\lt\delta\text{.}\) Then \(|f(x)-f(a)|\lt f(a)\text{,}\) so \(-f(a)\lt f(x)-f(a)\text{.}\) Adding \(f(a)\) to both sides gives \(f(x)\gt 0\text{,}\) as desired.
